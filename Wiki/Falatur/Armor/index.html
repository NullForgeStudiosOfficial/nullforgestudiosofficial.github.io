Armor Overview
Armor provides additional protection against incoming attacks, allowing players to withstand damage that would otherwise injure them. Falatur simplifies the many different forms of armor into two classifications: Light Armor and Heavy Armor. Armor is classified by its weight rather than the material from which it is constructed. This allows players to create a wide variety of armor designs without being restricted to specific materials. Each armor classification provides a different amount of Hit Points, which absorb damage before it reaches the player. Unlike individual limbs, armor does not track damage separately for each piece. Instead, all equipped armor shares a single Hit Point pool. Armor Classification A player's armor classification is determined by the lowest qualifying armor piece they have equipped that is outwardly visible. If any equipped armor piece belongs to a lower classification, the player's entire suit is considered to belong to that classification. For example, a player wearing a Heavy chest piece with Light arm protection is considered to be wearing Light Armor. Armor Inspection Armor must be inspected and approved by a Judge before being used in combat. A Judge will determine its classification based on the weight of its individual pieces. Players are responsible for ensuring their armor remains compliant with all applicable rules.
Armor Classes
Falatur divides armor into two classifications: Light Armor and Heavy Armor. Each classification has its own weight requirements and provides a different amount of protection. Armor is classified according to the weight of its individual pieces, not the materials used in its construction. Light Armor Light Armor consists of armor pieces that do not meet the minimum weight requirements for Heavy Armor. Light Armor provides 2 Armor Hit Points. Light Armor Weight Requirements • Chest: 0–6 pounds. • Arms: 0–4 pounds. • Legs: 0–4 pounds. Heavy Armor Heavy Armor consists of armor pieces that meet or exceed the minimum weight requirements for Heavy Armor. Heavy Armor provides 4 Armor Hit Points. Heavy Armor Weight Requirements • Chest: 7 pounds or more. • Arms: 5 pounds or more. • Legs: 5 pounds or more. Mixed Armor Classifications When a player wears armor pieces belonging to different classifications, their entire suit is classified according to the lowest qualifying armor piece that is outwardly visible. For example, a player wearing a 7-pound chest piece and 2-pound arm protection is considered to be wearing Light Armor. That player receives 2 Armor Hit Points rather than 4. To qualify for Heavy Armor, all qualifying outwardly visible armor pieces must meet the Heavy Armor weight requirements.
Armor Damage
Armor protects players by absorbing incoming damage before it reaches their Hit Points. All equipped armor shares a single Hit Point pool, determined by the player's Armor Classification. Armor Hit Points Light Armor provides 2 Armor Hit Points. Heavy Armor provides 4 Armor Hit Points. Armor Hit Points are shared across the entire suit, regardless of how many individual pieces are equipped. For example, a player wearing Heavy Armor who receives 2 damage to their armored chest has 2 Armor Hit Points remaining. If their next attack strikes an armored arm for another 2 damage, their armor is destroyed. Taking Damage When an attack strikes a location protected by armor, applicable damage is subtracted from the armor's remaining Hit Points. Damage to any protected location affects the entire suit. Attacks against locations not covered by armor damage the player normally. Damage Types Slashing Damage: Damages armor before affecting the player. Bludgeoning Damage: Damages armor before affecting the player. Piercing Damage: Bypasses armor entirely, dealing damage directly to the player. When an attack deals multiple Damage Types, each type is resolved according to its normal rules. Destroyed Armor When Armor Hit Points reach 0, the armor is considered destroyed. Destroyed armor no longer provides protection against incoming attacks. Excess Damage Armor absorbs the entirety of any applicable attack that strikes it, regardless of its remaining Hit Points. If an attack deals more damage than the armor has remaining, the armor is destroyed, but the excess damage does not carry over to the player. For example, a player wearing Light Armor with 2 Armor Hit Points remaining is struck by a Hammer dealing 4 Bludgeoning Damage. The armor is destroyed, but the player receives no damage from the attack. This also applies to attacks that would otherwise injure or Sever a limb. If the armor absorbs the attack, the protected limb is unaffected. Once armor is destroyed, subsequent attacks against the player are resolved normally, as though the armor were not present.
Armor Restrictions
Armor must meet all applicable classification and safety requirements before being used in combat. Falatur does not require armor to be constructed from specific materials or replicate any particular historical design. Players are encouraged to create armor that suits their preferences, provided it remains safe and complies with the rules. Weight Requirements Armor classification is determined by the weight of each individual armor piece rather than the combined weight of the entire suit. Players may not combine the weight of multiple pieces to meet the requirements of a higher Armor Classification. If any outwardly visible qualifying armor piece belongs to a lower classification, the entire suit is considered to belong to that classification. Armor Coverage Armor only protects the areas of the body it physically covers. Attacks striking exposed areas are resolved normally and do not reduce Armor Hit Points. Players may not claim armor protection for areas that are not actually covered by their equipment. Armor Construction Armor must be constructed without exposed sharp edges, hazardous protrusions, or other features that could reasonably injure another player. All armor must remain securely attached during combat. Armor may not contain intentionally dangerous components or modifications designed to harm opponents. Armor Inspection Armor must be inspected and approved by a Judge before being used in combat. Judges may examine individual armor pieces to verify their weight, classification, construction, and safety. Armor that fails inspection may not be used until the identified issues have been corrected. Damaged Armor Armor that becomes physically damaged during combat must remain safe to wear. If an armor piece develops dangerous edges, loose components, or other hazards, it must be removed or repaired before combat continues. Judge Discretion Judges have final authority regarding armor classification, construction, and safety. Armor that technically meets its weight requirements may still be rejected if its construction presents an unreasonable safety risk.
