Introduction
Falatur (FAL-uh-tur) is a Quenya-inspired name chosen to represent the ideals of the system rather than a direct translation. Drawing from Quenya roots associated with foam (fal-) and mastery (-tur), the name symbolizes the pursuit of excellence through the practice of foam combat. Falatur is founded on the belief that combat is not merely about victory, but about honor, discipline, craftsmanship, and continual self-improvement. Falatur is designed to be easy to learn, inexpensive to play, and difficult to master. These rules will do their best to adhere to that philosphy.
Rule of Honor
Falatur is built upon trust and honor. The purpose of the Rule of Honor is not to prevent cheating. It is to ensure that winning is never more important than integrity. Falatur depends more upon its players than its rules. Every player is both a competitor and an official. Without trust, no honor-based combat system can function. Every opponent trusts you with their safety. Honor that trust. Players are expected to acknowledge valid hits, respect their opponents, and uphold both the written rules and the spirit in which they were intended. No ruleset can account for every situation. When uncertainty arises, players are expected to resolve it honestly rather than seek advantage through technicalities. Winning through dishonesty is not a victory. If two players genuinely disagree, neither should assume the other is acting dishonestly. Resolve the disagreement respectfully or request a Judge. When in doubt, favor your opponent.
Participation
Players participate at their own risk. Foam combat is a physical activity involving running, falling, and controlled weapon strikes. Minor injuries such as bruises, scrapes, and muscle soreness should be expected. Clothing Players are encouraged to wear fantasy or historical clothing, but no costume is required unless specified by the event organizer. Clothing must allow safe movement and may not present a hazard to the player or others.
Safety
Judges Discretion The Judge has final authority over all safety decisions. Any equipment or action deemed unsafe may be prohibited, even if it technically complies with the written rules. Controlled Force Players must always fight with controlled force. Excessive force, reckless behavior, or intentionally attempting to injure another player is prohibited. Unsafe Conditions If a player believes an unsafe condition exists, they should immediately call "Hold!" All players must stop fighting immediately until a Judge declares the field safe. Respect The Field Players must be aware of hazards such as holes, uneven ground, rocks, low branches, spectators, and wildlife. The Judge may modify or stop play if the battlefield becomes unsafe.
Conduct
Blind Attacks A player may not intentionally strike a target they cannot reasonably see. A player's vision may not be intentionally obstructed by equipment. Physical Contact Intentional punching, kicking, elbowing, kneeing, or other abusive contact is prohibited unless explicitly allowed by the scenario. Incidental contact during normal combat is expected. Sportsmanship As outlined in the Rule of Honor: Players are expected to prioritize the safety of themselves and others above winning. If a situation feels unsafe, stop and ask a Judge. Due to this. No rule, objective, or victory is more important to a players safety.
Equipment Regulations
Safe Equipment All weapons, shields, and equipment must pass inspection before use. Construction requirements are described in Weapon Construction. Equipment that becomes unsafe during play must be removed from the field immediately. A Judge may inspect equipment at any time. Weapons may not be intentionally thrown unless they are specifically designed and approved as thrown weapons. Any significant modification to approved equipment requires reinspection before use. Modifications No equipment may be intentionally designed to exploit ambiguities or loopholes in these rules. The Judge's decision is final. Simulation Weapons should reasonably resemble the weapon they are intended to represent in both appearance and function. Equipment intentionally designed to confuse players or circumvent the rules is prohibited. This is not to be confused with unique weapons. Weapons must clearly fit within one of Falatur's weapon families. If not. A Judge will determine the weapon classification of any equipment whose function is unclear.
Judges
What Is A Judge Judges are responsible for interpreting and enforcing the rules of Falatur, resolving disputes, inspecting equipment, and maintaining the safety of all participants. Judges are expected to uphold the Rule of Honor to the same standard as every player. A Judge's responsibility is not to find a way to permit something simply because it technically satisfies the written rules. When making a decision, Judges must consider both the written rules and the purpose for which those rules exist. When in doubt, favor safety. No weapon, rule, objective, or victory is more important than the safety of a participant. Judge Authority Judges have final authority regarding safety, equipment legality, rule interpretation, and disputes during play. A Judge may prohibit any equipment, action, or behavior they reasonably believe presents an unsafe condition, even if it technically complies with the written rules. Judge discretion may be used to impose additional restrictions for safety. It may not be used to ignore or waive an explicit safety requirement. A Judge may stop play at any time if they believe an unsafe condition exists. Impartiality Judges must make decisions impartially and in accordance with the Rule of Honor. A Judge may not inspect their own equipment. A Judge should not make a ruling in which they have a significant conflict of interest when another Judge is reasonably available. Judges should never interpret an ambiguous rule for the purpose of granting themselves, their allies, or another player an unfair advantage. Equipment Inspection Equipment inspection must be performed by two Judges. Both Judges must independently determine that the equipment is safe before it may enter play. Either Judge may reject equipment for safety reasons. The owner of the equipment may explain its construction or intended function but may not perform the inspection themselves or substitute a self-test for independent inspection. Written construction requirements are minimum safety standards. Meeting those requirements does not guarantee that equipment will pass inspection. Judges should consider the equipment as a complete object, including its construction, structural integrity, padding, compression, flex, weight, and behavior during impact. Impact Testing When appropriate, weapons should undergo controlled impact testing as part of inspection. Impact testing must be performed between the inspecting Judges rather than by the weapon's owner. Testing should begin with controlled force and increase to force reasonably representative of normal combat. A weapon that cannot remain safe under the forces reasonably expected during play must not pass inspection. A weapon should not be approved merely because it can survive a gentle demonstration. Judges should inspect and test equipment according to how it will actually be used. During Play Judges may inspect previously approved equipment at any time. If equipment becomes damaged, degraded, loose, exposed, or otherwise potentially unsafe, a Judge may immediately remove it from play and require reinspection. Judges may call or enforce a "Hold!" whenever necessary to address an unsafe condition. Disputes When players cannot resolve a disagreement themselves under the Rule of Honor, they may request a Judge. Judges should hear the relevant players before making a ruling whenever reasonably possible. Not every situation can be explicitly addressed by the written rules. When interpretation is necessary, Judges should rule according to the intent of the rules, fairness to the participants, and participant safety. A Judge's ruling during play is final. A Judge is not there to help someone win. A Judge is there to ensure that everyone can safely and honorably continue playing.
Becoming a Judge
A player may only become a Judge when appointed by either: A Herald, or Two existing Judges(With approval from a Herald). Judges should only appoint players they trust to understand the rules, uphold the Rule of Honor, make impartial decisions, and prioritize player safety. Judge status is not earned automatically through experience, participation, or time spent playing Falatur.
Heralds
Heralds Heralds are the creators and maintainers of Falatur. A Herald may appoint a Judge without requiring the approval of other Judges. Heralds may revoke Judge status at any moment, for any reason they deem fit. Herald Exemptions Heralds are not exempt from any rules of Falatur and must adhere to them in the same manner as Judges and other players. The role of Judge or Herald merely increases responsibility; it does not remove restrictions. Both are still beholden to the Rule of Honor.
