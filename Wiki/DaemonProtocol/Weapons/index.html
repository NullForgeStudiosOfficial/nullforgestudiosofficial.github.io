Weapons
Weapons are the primary ways to deal damage in Daemon Protocol. However, choosing a weapon isn't simply a matter of finding whichever one deals the most damage. Every weapon has its own advantages, disadvantages, requirements, and limitations. Some are quick, some are powerful, and others offer greater flexibility at the cost of raw damage. Understanding how weapons function is just as important as knowing how to use them.
Action Points
Combat operates using "Action Points (AP)." Each combatant receives 30 AP per round, which can be spent attacking, moving, reloading, and performing other actions. Every weapon has an AP cost associated with attacking. This determines how frequently it can be used within a round. Movement also consumes AP, at a rate of 1 AP per tile. A powerful weapon may deal tremendous damage, but its AP cost could leave you with little room to reposition or perform additional actions.
Damage
Every weapon has a minimum and maximum base damage value. However, base damage is only part of the equation. Weapons also receive additional damage from the wielder's attributes. Different weapons benefit from different attributes, with some drawing from multiple attributes simultaneously. Generally, the damage bonus is calculated using the weapon's weighted attributes and its attack AP cost. Each AP spent contributes 3% of the relevant weighted attribute value to the attack's damage bonus. For example, a weapon costing 10 AP to attack receives a damage bonus equal to 30% of its weighted attribute value. Certain weapons have unique mechanics that modify how their damage is calculated. Weapons also possess damage types, which determine how their attacks interact with applicable combat mechanics.
Weapon Speed
Weapon Speed affects Initiative, not the number of attacks a weapon can perform. At the beginning of combat, initiative is determined using a d100 roll, modified by the equipped weapon's Speed. A positive Speed modifier improves initiative, while a negative modifier reduces it. Once combat begins, the weapon's AP costs determine how frequently it can attack. A weapon with high Speed isn't necessarily capable of attacking more frequently than a slower weapon.
Weapon Requirements
Some weapons have attribute requirements. These represent the attributes associated with properly wielding the weapon and are separate from its damage scaling. A weapon's requirements, damage scaling, and AP costs should all be considered when deciding whether it suits a particular character.
Weapon Proficiency
Characters develop proficiency through using weapons in combat. Proficiency is tracked by weapon family, allowing characters to improve their familiarity with particular styles of combat. Experience is earned through successful attacks: - Hit: 1 EXP - Critical Hit: 5 EXP - Kill: 10 EXP Each weapon family has its own proficiency progression and rewards, which are explained in their respective sections.
Weapon Upgrades
Weapons can also be upgraded to improve their base damage. Each upgrade increases the weapon's base damage by 15%, compounding with previous upgrades. This increase applies to the weapon's base damage, not the additional damage received from character attributes. Consequently, upgrading a weapon and improving the attributes it scales with are two separate methods of increasing its effectiveness.
Additional Properties
Depending on the weapon, additional considerations may include ammunition capacity, reloading costs, effective range, hand requirements, and unique attack mechanics. Not every weapon follows the same rules, and some possess properties that significantly alter how they function in combat. These differences are covered in the individual weapon family sections.
There is no single weapon designed to be the correct choice for every situation. A weapon's effectiveness depends on its wielder, the circumstances of combat, and how its strengths and weaknesses are utilized. Choose accordingly.
Weapon Cards
Weapons will be presented to you in the form of cards. You may click/tap them to flip them for more information.
One Handed Weapons
One-handed weapons offer a balance between damage, speed, and versatility. While they generally lack the raw power of larger two-handed weapons, their lower Action Point costs allow for more flexibility during combat. Their greatest advantage is the ability to keep one hand free, allowing the wielder to equip an additional weapon, shield, or other compatible equipment. Each weapon offers different strengths and weaknesses, making one-handed weapons suitable for a variety of combat styles.

Two Handed Weapons
Two-handed weapons prioritize raw power and damage output over speed and versatility. Generally requiring more Action Points to attack, these weapons deliver powerful strikes at the cost of fewer opportunities to act during combat. Their greatest disadvantage is the commitment of both hands, preventing the wielder from simultaneously equipping a shield or another weapon. While their attacks can be devastating, positioning, timing, and careful management of Action Points are essential to using them effectively.
