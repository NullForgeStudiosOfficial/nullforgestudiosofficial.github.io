The Ardron Universe Lore Intro
Welcome to the Lore of Ardron Universe. Not every story will be told here. Many characters have their own tales to share, and some of those stories are best experienced through gameplay. Instead, this archive presents the history of Ardron Universe as told through the eyes of its six gods. Their experiences. Their perspectives. Their versions of the truth.
Informational Backgrounds
Throughout this archive, the background color will change to indicate which god is currently speaking. Red represents Iwata. Green represents Sythys. Black represents Entar. Blue represents Zykius. Silver represents Astus. White represents Tynos. Purple represents... Someone. Remember: A god's perspective is not necessarily the whole truth. Nor is their memory perfect. You have been warned.
Imperfect Memory
Some chapters are Unknown. Lost to time. The gods didn't record it, or the records were destroyed. These may be recovered with time...with the help of others. Who's to know... but the Void knows the truth.
Work In Progress
The lore for Ardron is a heavy Work In Progress. Not so much that it isn't done... Just writing it all out. Remembering how it all goes, conversations, places, time. All that requires an immense amount of focus, and privacy. Something I don't necessarily have a lot of at the moment. Please bare with me.
Chapter 0: The Six
My Children...
Tynos...
Oh, poor Tynos. Your beginnings were not so grand, were they? When I created you, I had such higher hopes for you. Although, I suppose that is rather unfair of me to say. I had no particular expectations of what you would become. I merely manifested you into the Void, curious to see what you might accomplish. Even I did not know how you would fare. And yet, I cannot help but feel responsible for what happened. You see, the Void is not particularly forgiving to Voidlings. I knew this, of course. I had hoped that placing you near others would improve your chances. Alas... Perhaps I should explain. When I first manifested Tynos, I placed him relatively close to a group of eight Voidlings who had already found one another. I did not tell him where to go. I did not give him instructions, nor did I force him to join them. I simply... encouraged a feeling. A little curiosity. A little desire to travel in a particular direction. Such a small influence, really. And eventually, there he was. Among eight others, no longer alone. How wonderful. It did not take long for Tynos to appoint himself their protector. Nobody had asked this of him, mind you. He simply decided that their safety was his responsibility. Even then, long before White Mana, long before the Realms, and certainly long before anyone thought to call him a god... Tynos wished to maintain order. And for a time, he succeeded. Until one rather unfortunate incident. You see, Tynos had taken it upon himself to watch over the others while they rested. A noble gesture, certainly. But even Voidlings must rest. And so, during one of his watches, Tynos fell asleep. For approximately ten human seconds. Ten seconds! A rather insignificant amount of time, wouldn't you agree? Unfortunately, Tynos did not see it that way. When he awoke, the others had begun drifting apart. Not terribly far, mind you. Nobody had disappeared. Nobody was in any immediate danger. But Tynos panicked. He hurriedly gathered everyone together, arranging them into a circle, ensuring that nobody could drift away unnoticed. There. Everyone was safe. No harm done. Or so I thought. For Tynos, those ten seconds had become something far greater than a moment of sleep. They had become a failure. He had stopped watching, and the others had drifted apart. What if he had slept longer? What if someone had disappeared? What if he had never found them again? Oh, Tynos... You were all still there. But I suppose knowing that did little to comfort him. From that moment onward, he became increasingly reluctant to sleep while the others rested. He watched. And watched. And watched. Eventually, another Voidling offered to take his place. And after some time, Tynos finally allowed himself to rest. How unfortunate that he chose that particular moment to trust someone else. Far away, a star had reached the end of its existence. Its death sent a tremendous wave through the Void, disturbing everything in its path. Including our little group. The Voidlings were scattered. Their appointed watcher desperately attempted to gather everyone together, but amid the confusion, Tynos had drifted away from the others. Not so far that he could no longer see them. Not so far that they could not hear him. But far enough. When Tynos awoke, he saw the others in the distance. Naturally, he called out. The others were already frightened. Their protector was missing, and they were struggling to find him. But then they heard his voice. There he was! They turned toward him. Oh... If only they had continued looking elsewhere. You see, the dying star had disturbed something else. Something that had been resting within the Void. A creature. And while those seven Voidlings turned their attention toward Tynos... The creature consumed them. Just like that. Seven lives, gone. And Tynos... poor Tynos. He watched every moment. I wonder how long he remained there afterward. I wonder what thoughts passed through his mind. Did he blame the creature? The dying star? The Voidling who had taken his watch? No... I suspect you already know the answer. He blamed himself. After all, had he not fallen asleep, perhaps he could have prevented it. Had he remained awake, perhaps he could have gathered them together. Had he never trusted another to take his place... Perhaps. Such a troublesome word, isn't it? Perhaps. I watched Tynos change after that day. He had always been protective, certainly. But now his caution had become something else entirely. Every uncertainty was a potential disaster. Every mistake, an unacceptable risk. Every moment spent without control, an opportunity for something terrible to happen. And so he sought control wherever he could find it. He would rather exhaust himself than entrust another with responsibility. He would rather follow what was proven than risk discovering something new. And above all, he would rather suffer himself than allow those depending on him to suffer for his mistakes. An admirable quality, perhaps. Though taken to such extremes... Well. You will see. For you see, dear Tynos, your greatest mistake was yet to come. And unlike those ten seconds of sleep... That mistake would cost considerably more than seven lives. But we are getting ahead of ourselves, aren't we? There are five others whose beginnings deserve our attention. And I would hate to keep my children waiting.
